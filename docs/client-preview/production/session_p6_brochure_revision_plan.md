# Session P6 Revision — Client Brochure and Offer

**Date:** 16 August 2026  
**Status:** Complete internally; ready for owner inspection  
**Scope:** Rewrite and redesign the P6 client brochure and one-page offer; preserve the approved report, Excel workbook, brand direction, price, service boundaries, and manufactured-demo status

---

## 1. Why this revision exists

The first P6 brochure is visually sound but asks the client to read too much. It also exposes internal framing that does not belong in a sales conversation. Phrases such as “founding client,” “pilot,” and “monthly billing cycle” make a simple offer feel procedural.

The revised P6 must feel like a calm, premium service overview. A prospect should understand the service, see the two outputs, understand how products are shortlisted, and grasp the monthly offer without needing the presenter to translate the page.

## 2. Measured baseline and fixed targets

The first brochure contains 876 extracted words across six pages. The first offer summary contains 274 extracted words.

The revised limits are:

| Item | Maximum | Intended result |
|---|---:|---|
| Brochure length | 5 pages | One less transition and no repeated closing page |
| Brochure total | 520 words | At least 40% less copy |
| Page 1 | 32 words | A true cover, not an introduction page |
| Page 2 | 90 words | Explain the two outputs visually |
| Page 3 | 135 words | Explain selection in everyday language |
| Page 4 | 95 words | Demonstrate the sample without technical statistics |
| Page 5 | 168 words | State the whole offer once and clearly |
| Offer summary | 190 words | A scan-friendly one-page leave-behind |

These are ceilings, not targets to fill. Shorter copy is preferred when meaning remains complete.

## 3. Client-language contract

The brochure and offer must not use:

- founding client, founding-client, or founding offer;
- pilot or paid pilot;
- billing cycle or monthly cycle;
- commercial gate or commercial acceptance;
- operator-reviewed;
- core signal, evidence family, provider benchmark, deterministic, or initial service checks.

Use these natural replacements:

- “₹1,500 per month”;
- “Free setup”;
- “Paid monthly in advance”;
- “Four weekly PDF-and-Excel reports each month”;
- “Price fixed for the first two months”;
- “Reviewed before delivery”;
- “Choose a start date and confirm the service with NeuraFlow.”

The internal walkthrough may explain the commitment boundary, but its suggested client talk track must follow the same plain-language rules.

## 4. Revised page plan

### Page 1 — Product Opportunity Radar

- Service name and one-line value promise.
- One brief supporting sentence.
- NeuraFlow logo and the approved editorial still life.
- No price, method detail, disclaimer, feature list, or sales terminology.

### Page 2 — What you receive every week

- One large mobile-report preview and one large Excel preview.
- Two short captions: the PDF helps the client decide; Excel shows the evidence.
- Four simple facts: four reports, Monday delivery, reviewed before delivery, PDF plus Excel.
- No explanation of source architecture or data generation.

### Page 3 — How products are shortlisted

- Three plain signal rows: advertising activity, marketplace visibility, and Google search interest.
- One sentence explaining that agreement across more signals creates a stronger opportunity.
- A compact context row for weekly change, quick-commerce availability, possible supplier leads, and review flags.
- One honest boundary: weak products are not added merely to fill a list.

### Page 4 — See the output in action

- Four understandable sample facts: 12 opportunities, four categories, five review flags, and two formats.
- One report crop and one Excel crop.
- Four brief proof labels: weekly change, visible gaps, supplier starting points, client review reminders.
- The single brochure disclaimer: “Illustrative preview using sample data; not a live market-research report.”

### Page 5 — Simple monthly service

- Large “₹1,500 per month” and “Free setup.”
- Four weekly reports and Monday delivery.
- A concise included list and concise limits.
- Tax wording and delivery method once.
- One short responsibility boundary.
- One direct next step without urgency or internal approval language.

## 5. Visual-density rules

- Let the existing images carry the demonstration; do not explain what can already be seen.
- Use generous open space and a clear reading path rather than filling each panel.
- Use dividers only to separate genuinely different information.
- Keep one primary headline, one short introduction, and no more than two supporting content groups per page.
- Avoid card walls, repeated labels, duplicate facts, long bullets, decorative jargon, price stickers, gradients, and artificial urgency.
- Maintain the approved cream, maroon, muted navy, sage, rose, and mustard family.
- Retain the single line beneath each page number; do not add extra folio decoration.

## 6. Facts that must remain exact

- Price: ₹1,500 per month.
- Payment: paid monthly in advance.
- Setup: free.
- Cadence: four weekly PDF-and-Excel reports each month.
- Normal delivery day: Monday.
- Delivery: email with a WhatsApp notification and download link.
- Price is fixed for the first two months.
- Up to 12 eligible opportunities; fewer is valid when evidence is weaker.
- Taxes: “Plus legally applicable taxes, if any.”
- Supplier leads are starting points and are not verified suppliers.
- The client remains responsible for product selection, compliance, supplier checks, sampling, landed cost, pricing, and margin.

## 7. Pre-production critique

### Risk: cutting copy makes the brochure vague

**Correction:** Keep the five client decisions intact: what it is, what arrives, how selection works, what the output looks like, and what it costs. Remove implementation detail, not meaning.

### Risk: the evidence method sounds childish after simplification

**Correction:** Use accurate everyday language. Explain the three market views and their agreement without exposing scoring terminology that a prospect does not need.

### Risk: the offer page repeats the separate offer sheet

**Correction:** The brochure page provides the decision summary. The separate offer sheet is the concise leave-behind with the same facts, generated from the same configuration.

### Risk: a sparse layout feels unfinished

**Correction:** Preserve strong image scale, intentional spacing, restrained colour blocks, and useful hierarchy. Empty space must frame the message rather than look accidental.

### Risk: the demo disclaimer is hidden or repeated

**Correction:** Place it once, directly under the sample-output demonstration on page 4. Do not repeat it on other brochure pages or on the offer sheet.

### Risk: plain language weakens important boundaries

**Correction:** Keep one direct sentence about supplier verification and one concise client-responsibility sentence. Move detailed explanations to the presenter guide and the full sample outputs.

## 8. Production and review loop

1. Freeze the revised commercial wording in the configuration file.
2. Rewrite the brochure into five pages and simplify the one-page offer.
3. Update filenames, metadata, presenter guidance, package guide, and manifest together.
4. Add automated checks for page count, word ceilings, exact commercial facts, disclosure placement, banned terminology, supplier boundaries, and file integrity.
5. Generate the HTML/CSS sources and both PDFs.
6. Render every client-facing page to images inside `media/client-preview/`.
7. Inspect the five-page overview, each full-size page, and the offer at mobile-like scale.
8. Correct copy density, overflow, contrast, alignment, image crops, hierarchy, repeated information, and unnatural language.
9. Repeat generation, validation, rendering, and visual inspection until no known issue remains.
10. Move the first P6 PDFs into a recoverable archive and write the revised review renders to a clearly labelled new media folder so the two review sets cannot be confused.

## 9. Exit criteria

The revision is ready for owner review only when:

- the brochure has exactly five A4 portrait pages and the offer has one;
- all word ceilings pass automatically;
- no banned client language appears in either client-facing PDF or its HTML source;
- shared price, delivery, scope, and boundary facts match exactly;
- the disclaimer appears once on brochure page 4 and nowhere in the offer;
- all pages have selectable text, correct metadata, valid local images, and no placeholders;
- review images show no clipping, crowding, weak contrast, tiny essential copy, or accidental blank space;
- the package guide and manifest point only to the revised client-facing filenames;
- the approved P4 Excel and P5 report remain unchanged.
